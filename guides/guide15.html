🚨 Emergency Digital Audit Checklist

Review important accounts and security settings in one pass.

Why it matters

If you think an account was hacked, or you just want to be safe after a data breach, you can fix most of the risk in one session of about an hour. Go in this order, because your email account is the key to resetting everything else.

Steps

  1. Secure your main email first. Change its password to a new long, unique one and turn on two-factor authentication.
  2. Sign out of all other devices and sessions in the account's security settings, then check the recovery email and phone number are yours.
  3. Check for mail rules. Look for forwarding rules or filters you did not create, because attackers use them to spy on your email.
  4. Check if your email was in a breach at haveibeenpwned.com and change the passwords of any account listed.
  5. Change passwords on your most important accounts: banking, cloud storage, social media, shopping and your phone account. Use your password manager.
  6. Turn on 2FA everywhere it is offered, and save your backup codes.
  7. Review connected apps and logged-in devices and remove anything you do not recognize.
  8. Update your devices (phone, computer, router) and run a scan with a trusted security tool.
  9. Watch your money. Check bank and card statements, and contact your bank if you see anything strange. Consider freezing your cards.
  10. Warn your contacts if the hacker may have messaged them, and report scams to the proper authorities.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Do this from a device you trust, not the one you think is infected.
  • Write down what you changed, so you do not miss any accounts.
  • After the emergency, set a reminder to repeat this checklist twice a year.
