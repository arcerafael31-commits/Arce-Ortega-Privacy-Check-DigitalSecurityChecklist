🛡️ Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) 101

Add another layer of protection to your accounts.

Why it matters

A password can be guessed, leaked or tricked out of you. Two-factor authentication (2FA, also called multifactor authentication or MFA) asks for a second proof, such as a code or a security key, so a stolen password alone is not enough to get in. CISA says any MFA is better than none, but some types are stronger than others.

Steps

  1. Make a priority list. Start with your main email account, then banking, cloud storage, social media and your password manager.
  2. Open the account's security settings and look for "2-step verification", "two-factor authentication" or "MFA".
  3. Choose the strongest method offered. From strongest to weakest: security keys or passkeys (phishing-resistant), then authenticator apps, then codes sent by text or email.
  4. Set up an authenticator app (for example, one from Microsoft, Google or an open-source app) by scanning the QR code the website shows. Use an app that lets you back up or transfer codes to a new phone.
  5. Save your backup codes and store them offline, for example printed in a safe place.
  6. Add a second method, such as a spare security key or another trusted device, in case you lose your phone.
  7. Test it. Sign out and sign back in to make sure the second step works before you need it.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Never read your code to anyone. A real company will not ask you for it.
  • Text-message codes are better than nothing, but they can be intercepted, so switch to an app or key when you can.
  • Before replacing your phone, move your authenticator app first, or you may be locked out.
