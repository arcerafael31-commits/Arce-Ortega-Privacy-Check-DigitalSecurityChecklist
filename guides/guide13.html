🎣 Avoiding Phishing Scams

Recognize suspicious messages, links, and attachments.

Why it matters

Phishing is when a scammer pretends to be a company, bank, delivery service or someone you know to trick you into clicking a bad link, opening an attachment or giving up a password, code or money. It arrives by email, text, calls, social media and even QR codes.

Steps

  1. Learn the red flags. The FTC lists: unexpected messages from a company you know, claims of an overdue invoice or a problem you must fix, requests to confirm a card or bank account number, pressure to click a link or open an attachment, and requests for details like your date of birth or ID numbers.
  2. Stop and think. Scammers want you to act in a hurry. Slow down.
  3. Do not click links or download attachments in unexpected messages.
  4. Check with the real company. Contact them using a phone number, email or website you already know is real, not the one in the message.
  5. Look at the details: the sender's real email address, spelling mistakes, and where a link goes (hover over it or press and hold it on a phone).
  6. Never share one-time codes or passwords, even with someone who says they are from support.
  7. Keep spam filters on, mark spam as junk, and turn on two-factor authentication to limit the damage if your password is stolen.
  8. Report it. Forward phishing emails to reportphishing@apwg.org and report to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov (US), or to your local cybercrime authority, then delete the message.

Tips and common mistakes

  • If you already clicked or shared something: change the password right away from a safe device, turn on 2FA, contact your bank if money or cards were involved, and watch your accounts.
  • Scammers can copy logos and names perfectly, so judge the message by what it asks you to do, not how it looks.
