🗂️ Data Broker Opt-Out Guide

Learn how to limit the sale and sharing of personal data.

Why it matters

Data brokers collect personal details such as your name, address, phone number, age and shopping habits, and sell them. This data is used for ads, but also by scammers and people-search websites. You cannot remove yourself from everything, but you can reduce how much is out there.

Steps

  1. Search for yourself (your name with your city) to see which people-search sites list you.
  2. Find the opt-out page on each site, usually in the footer as "Do Not Sell or Share" or "Opt Out", and submit the request. Use an email alias if you can.
  3. If you live in California, use DROP. The state's Delete Request and Opt-out Platform at privacy.ca.gov/drop lets you send one deletion request to all registered data brokers. Save your DROP ID. Brokers must start processing DROP requests from August 1, 2026, and have 90 days to act.
  4. Outside California, there is usually no single tool. Some US states (such as Oregon, Texas and Vermont) require brokers to register, but you still send requests to each broker.
  5. In the Philippines, the Data Privacy Act of 2012 gives you rights over your personal data, including to access it, correct it and object to or request blocking or removal of it. Send your request to the company's data protection officer, and contact the National Privacy Commission if it is ignored.
  6. Keep a list of the sites you opted out of and the date, and check again every few months, because data tends to reappear.
  7. Reduce new data: share less on forms, surveys and loyalty programs, and tighten social media privacy settings.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Paid removal services exist, but they cost money and you can do the same steps for free.
  • Only use opt-out links on the broker's real website, and never pay or give extra personal details to a stranger who contacts you.
  • Rules and tools change, so check the official pages for your region.
