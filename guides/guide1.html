🔐 Choosing a Password Manager

Store and manage your passwords securely.

Why it matters

Most people reuse the same few passwords. When one website is breached, attackers try that password on your email, bank and social accounts. A password manager creates and remembers a long, different password for every account, so you only have to memorize one.

Steps

  1. Pick a manager. Built-in options (Apple Passwords, Google Password Manager) are fine for beginners. Standalone options include Bitwarden, 1Password and KeePassXC. Look for strong encryption, independent security audits, support for all your devices, and the ability to export your data.
  2. Create one strong master password. Use a passphrase of several random words, at least 16 characters long, that you have never used anywhere else.
  3. Protect the manager itself. Turn on two-factor authentication for the manager account and keep the recovery key or emergency kit printed or written down somewhere safe.
  4. Install it everywhere. Add the app on your phone and the extension in your browser so passwords fill in automatically.
  5. Start with the accounts that matter most: your email, banking, cloud storage and social media. Replace their passwords with generated ones of 16 or more random characters.
  6. Fix the rest over time. Use the manager's security report to find reused, weak or breached passwords and change a few each week.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Autofill only works on the real website address. If it does not fill in, check the address bar, because it may be a fake site.
  • Never store your master password in a plain note on your phone or in the browser.
  • Do not share passwords by chat or text.
