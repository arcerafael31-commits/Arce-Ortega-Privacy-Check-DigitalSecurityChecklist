📶 Securing Your Router

Protect your home Wi-Fi network and router settings.

Why it matters

Your router is the front door to your home network. If an attacker controls it, they can watch your traffic, redirect you to fake websites or attack your devices. Many routers are left on factory settings for years.

Steps

  1. Open the router's settings page. The address and the default login are usually on a label on the router, or in your internet provider's app.
  2. Change the router login. Factory passwords are often publicly known, so use a long, random, unique one.
  3. Update the firmware and turn on automatic updates if the router has the option.
  4. Rename your Wi-Fi network so it does not show the router model or any personal information.
  5. Set a strong Wi-Fi passphrase of 5 to 7 unrelated words (at least 16 characters) that you do not use anywhere else.
  6. Use WPA3 Personal or WPA2 with AES. Open networks and older types such as WEP, WPA or WPA2-TKIP are not secure.
  7. Turn off remote management, WPS and UPnP. These features make it easier for outsiders to get in.
  8. Use a guest network for visitors and for smart-home devices, so they cannot reach your main devices.

Tips and common mistakes

  • If your router is too old to get updates or cannot use WPA2/WPA3, ask your provider for a newer one.
  • Keep the router in a safe place. Anyone who can touch it can factory-reset it back to the default settings.
