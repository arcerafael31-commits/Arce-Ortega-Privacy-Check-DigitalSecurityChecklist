🛰️ VPNs Explained

Understand when a VPN can help protect your online privacy.

Why it matters

A VPN (virtual private network) encrypts your traffic between your device and the VPN company's server. It can protect your activity on public Wi-Fi and hide it from your local network and internet provider. It does not make you anonymous: you are trusting the VPN company instead, and it does not stop phishing or malware.

Steps

  1. Decide why you need one. Good reasons: using public Wi-Fi, or keeping your internet provider from seeing which sites you visit. A VPN is not a fix for every privacy problem.
  2. Research the provider. The FTC advises you to research the app, check the permissions it asks for, confirm it encrypts your information, and check whether it shares your information with third parties.
  3. Read the privacy policy for what it logs. Be careful with free VPNs, because they may earn money from your data or ads.
  4. Install it only from the official app store or the company's website.
  5. Turn on the "kill switch" (if offered), which blocks traffic if the VPN drops, and set the app to connect automatically on untrusted networks.
  6. Test that it works by checking that your visible IP address changes when it is connected.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Keep using HTTPS websites, strong passwords and 2FA. A VPN does not replace them.
  • Websites can still recognize you if you are logged in or if your browser is fingerprinted.
  • Some websites and apps block VPN connections.
