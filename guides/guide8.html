✉️ Email Aliases & Masking

Reduce exposure of your main email address.

Why it matters

Every website you give your real email address to can sell it, leak it or send you spam. An email alias is a different address that forwards to your real inbox. If one alias starts getting spam or is leaked, you can turn it off without losing your main address.

Steps

  1. Choose a service. Examples: Apple's Hide My Email (with iCloud+), Firefox Relay, SimpleLogin, or DuckDuckGo Email Protection.
  2. Set it up and link it to your real inbox. For Hide My Email on iPhone, go to Settings, your name, iCloud, Hide My Email.
  3. Make a new alias for each website or app, and give it a label such as the company's name so you remember where it is used.
  4. Use aliases for shopping, newsletters, free trials and new accounts where you do not need to be contacted for important reasons.
  5. Keep your real address for important accounts such as banking, government and work.
  6. If an alias gets spam, you know which company leaked or sold it. Delete or deactivate that alias.
  7. Protect the alias account itself with a strong password and 2FA, and note recovery steps, because losing access could lock you out of accounts that use the aliases.

Tips and common mistakes

  • Adding "+name" to a Gmail address (like you+shop@gmail.com) helps with filtering, but it is easy for a company to strip off, so it is not real masking.
  • Aliases hide your address, not your identity. Anything else you give the website can still identify you.
